# UNDERPRICING SELF-AUDIT
## 15 Signs You're Charging Less Than Your Work Is Worth

**How to use this audit:** Answer all 15 questions honestly — score 1 point for every YES. Total your score at the end and read your result. Do this quarterly: pricing drifts downward silently, and a 10-minute audit catches it before a year of underpaid work does.

**Scoring rule:** Count your YES answers.
- **0–3: Healthy.** Your pricing is broadly sound. Re-audit in 3 months.
- **4–7: Leaking.** You're leaving meaningful money on the table in specific areas. Fix your 3 highest-impact YES items this month.
- **8–11: Underpriced.** Your rates are materially below your value. Run the calculator sheets, raise rates on new inquiries immediately, and send increase emails to existing clients within 30 days.
- **12–15: Emergency.** You are subsidizing your clients' businesses with your own financial security. Stop taking new work until you've repriced — every project you sign at current rates deepens the hole.

## THE 15 QUESTIONS

**Signs in your numbers (margin math):**

1. You cannot state your current effective hourly rate (total income ÷ total hours worked, including admin) from memory. *If you don't know the number, you can't manage it.*
2. Your effective hourly rate is below the floor rate on your Cost-Plus sheet. *You're working for less than your costs-plus-margin minimum.*
3. You haven't raised your rates in over 12 months. *Your costs rose; if your rates didn't, you gave yourself a pay cut.*
4. More than 30% of your working time is non-billable, but your rate doesn't account for it. *Check the Utilization-Adjusted sheet — unbilled hours are a hidden discount.*
5. You regularly work evenings or weekends to "catch up" on fixed-price projects. *The project was underquoted; you're donating the overrun.*

**Signs in your client interactions:**

6. Clients never push back on your quotes — every quote is accepted instantly. *Zero resistance usually means you're the cheapest option they found, not the best.*
7. You've discounted your rate to win work in the last 3 months. *Discounting to win is borrowing from your future self at high interest.*
8. A client has asked you to lower your price *after* agreeing to it. *And you said yes — which taught them to ask every time.*
9. You feel a flash of resentment when a "quick favor" request arrives from a client. *Resentment is your margin telling you the truth about that relationship.*
10. You avoid sending the invoice, or feel guilty when you do. *If getting paid feels wrong, your pricing psychology needs the reset this kit provides.*

**Signs in your client quality (the correlation):**

11. Your most demanding, slowest-paying clients are also your cheapest ones. *Low prices attract high-maintenance clients — this correlation is the most reliable signal in freelancing.*
12. Your best clients — the ones who pay on time and respect your time — came through referrals or premium channels, not bargain hunting. *Proof that better clients exist at better rates.*
13. You've lost a bid and later learned the winner charged *more* than you. *The client didn't want cheap; they wanted confidence. Your low quote signaled risk.*
14. You can't afford to turn down bad-fit work. *When every inquiry is load-bearing, you can't say no — and clients can smell it.*
15. Your income has been flat for a year or more despite working the same (or more) hours. *Flat income + same hours = your real rate is declining against inflation and rising costs.*

## AFTER YOU SCORE

**If you scored 4+:** Open the calculator workbook and complete all four sheets with honest numbers — not aspirational ones, real ones. Your recommended rate on the Annual Back-Solve sheet is your new starting point for every new inquiry from today.

**Then do these three things this week:**
1. **New inquiries:** Quote the new rate. No transition period for strangers — they never knew the old price.
2. **Existing clients:** Pick the right email variant from the rate-increase scripts and send it. Start with the client you'd least mind losing; the first send is the hardest.
3. **Scope discipline:** Add revision counts and change-order language to your next fixed quote (see the quoting decision tree). Half of underpricing isn't the rate — it's the unbilled extra work around it.

**Re-audit date:** Write it down now — 90 days from today. Pricing is a practice, not a one-time fix.
